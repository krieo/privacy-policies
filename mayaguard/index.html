1. Introduction

This Privacy Policy explains how MayaGuard ("the App", "we", "our") handles your information. MayaGuard is a privacy-first app lock and password vault for Android.

By using the App, you agree to the practices described in this policy. If you do not agree, please do not use the App.

2. Information We Collect

2.1 Information You Provide

The App allows you to lock apps, store passwords in an encrypted vault, and capture intruder photos. All data you enter is stored locally on your device and is never sent to our servers (we do not operate any servers).

This includes:

  • Your list of installed or locked apps
  • Vault entries (passwords, notes, subscriptions)
  • Intruder photos and metadata
  • Unlock attempts and usage patterns
  • PIN, pattern, or biometric preferences
  • App settings and theme preferences

Everything above lives in app-private storage on your device and is wiped when you uninstall.

3. How Your Vault Is Protected

Vault entries are encrypted with AES-256-GCM, the same authenticated-encryption standard used by HTTPS. The encryption key is derived from your vault password using Argon2id — a memory-hard key-derivation function (64 MB, 3 iterations, 4 threads) chosen to make brute-force attacks infeasible even on specialised hardware.

Both the password and the derived key are wiped from memory after use, and neither ever leaves your device.

We cannot read your vault — not even if you send us a bug report — because we have no access to the key and no copy of the data. If you forget your vault password there is no reset: the data is mathematically unrecoverable. That is the intended design.

4. Intruder Photos

If you enable the intruder capture feature, the front camera takes a photo after a configurable number of failed unlock attempts. These photos are:

  • Saved to app-private storage on your device
  • Never uploaded anywhere
  • Viewable and deletable at any time in Security → Intruder Gallery

Uninstalling MayaGuard deletes all captured photos.

6. Advertising

MayaGuard shows ads from Google AdMob to keep the app free. Ads appear sparingly — you will not see an ad on every launch. When ads are shown, Google's AdMob SDK may collect:

  • Your device's Advertising ID
  • Approximate location (derived from IP address)
  • General device information (model, OS version)
  • Ad interaction data (views, clicks)

This data is collected and processed by Google in accordance with Google's Privacy Policy.

You can opt out of personalized advertising by adjusting your device's ad settings: Settings → Google → Ads → Opt out of Ads Personalization.

Ads are never shown during active security flows (lock overlay, biometric gate, intruder capture). Those are trust-critical paths where ads would compromise the user experience.

7. Breach Checking

When you use the password breach check feature, a partial hash of your password (the first 5 characters of a SHA-1 hash) is sent to the Have I Been Pwned API using their k-anonymity model. Your actual password is never transmitted — only a short hash prefix that matches thousands of other passwords, making it impossible for the service to identify yours.

8. Data Storage and Security

  • All app lock settings and preferences are stored using Android's DataStore
  • Vault entries are encrypted with AES-256-GCM before storage
  • Intruder photos are stored in the App's private storage directory
  • We do not transmit any data to external servers
  • The App has android:allowBackup="false" to prevent Android system backups of app data

9. Data Retention and Deletion

  • Local data: Stored until you delete it or uninstall the App
  • Intruder photos: Stored until you delete them via Intruder Gallery, or uninstall the App
  • Vault entries: Stored until you delete them within the App, or uninstall
  • Uninstalling the App: Removes all local data (database, vault, intruder photos, preferences) from your device permanently
